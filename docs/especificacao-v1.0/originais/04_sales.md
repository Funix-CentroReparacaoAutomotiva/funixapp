# FUNIX — Sales Operating System

**Release and approvals:** Consult `FUNIX_REGISTROS_VIVOS.md` for the approved release, current commercial facts, pricing authority and open decisions. This document is a **v0.3.1 proposal**, not a claim of an approved pricing policy or deployed CRM functionality.

## 1. Purpose and boundaries

This file owns the **commercial process**: lead response, triage, quotation, price conversations, quote comparisons, discount procedure, customer authorization, handoff and follow-up. It does not set accounting cost formulas (`02_business_model.md`), technical acceptance criteria or capacity (`05_operations.md`), customer research conclusions (`03_customer_psychology.md`), legal rights (`06_legal_and_policy.md`), software schemas (`07_digital.md`), public creative standards (`08_brand_assets.md`), or enterprise priorities (`10_strategy.md`). Use the precedence and information-status rules in `00_master_index.md` and the decision method in `09_decision_framework.md`.

This is an operating philosophy and minimum control model, **not** a message-script library, a validated price list or a complete CRM specification.

## 2. Commercial objective and customer promise

**Objective:** Convert appropriate demand into **approved, executable repairs with sustainable economics**, clear customer expectations and consistent technical quality. Higher quote volume, conversion, ticket size or shop occupancy alone do not establish success.

Preserve the original principle: **guide first, execute second**. In practice, guide **when guidance helps** and **quote promptly when information is sufficient**. A straightforward request must not become a long consultation simply to appear professional. An uncertain or safety-sensitive repair must not be quoted with fabricated confidence simply to appear fast.

The customer should experience a capable, accessible, pragmatic **“amigo engenheiro”**: an expert who solves the problem plainly, explains what matters and makes next steps easy. FUNIX should not signal luxury, exclusivity or discount-led cheapness. **Highly competitive price is an intended result to verify, not a universal external claim.**

## 3. Customer fit and commercial qualification

A customer can care deeply about quality **and** compare prices aggressively. Neither price sensitivity nor a competitor quote is evidence of bad fit. Do not infer psychological motives without evidence; `03` owns behavioral hypotheses.

Qualify on separately assessed grounds:

- **Technical:** service and method can meet safety, scope and quality requirements (`05`, `06`).
- **Customer need:** the proposed work solves the actual need, with an understandable result and exclusions.
- **Economic:** the job can meet the presently defensible cost/contribution conditions (`02`); unknown cost is flagged, not silently treated as positive margin.
- **Capacity and timing:** production can responsibly accept the work given current commitments, parts and bottlenecks (`05`).
- **Integrity and authorization:** expectations, consent and commercial conduct are workable (`01`, `06`).

A job may be declined, deferred, redirected or redesigned only for **grounded technical, economic, capacity, legal or conduct reasons**. Do not disqualify based simply on vehicle value, budget, bargaining or quote comparison.

## 4. Commercial flow: proportional, visible, owned

The normal path consists of:

1. **Entry and triage:** identify request, lead channel where available, urgency, insurance/external dependencies and next action.
2. **First useful response:** acknowledge the actual issue and move the case forward; automation-only greeting is not automatically a useful response.
3. **Minimum information:** collect the vehicle and damage context needed for the next decision, photos if helpful and the relevant contact. Do not require unnecessary full registration at first contact.
4. **Choose route:** direct quote when evidence and approved method suffice; preliminary technical guidance when appropriate; inspection when hidden damage, risk, finish, scope or price materially depends on it.
5. **Prepare quotation:** define scope, commercial service(s), price, payment terms, limitations and next step, with technical approval where needed.
6. **Discuss alternatives and objections:** compare equivalent offers, identify real barriers and negotiate only within authority.
7. **Obtain explicit approval:** capture what was agreed, its version, relevant assumptions and authorization.
8. **Handoff and schedule:** transmit approved work and promises to operations; do not treat acceptance as immediate capacity.
9. **Follow up and close outcome:** progress, defer, decline, expire or complete; retain reliable reasons and learning.

Not every step needs its own interaction. Each case should have an identifiable **stage, accountable next action, pending dependency and outcome**; exact CRM status names belong to the system specification in `07`.

**Response-time benchmark:** the former **under-ten-minute** goal during business hours is **historical and unverified**, not a publicly guaranteed SLA. Measure first *useful* response separately from automatic acknowledgment; adapt staffing or target based on evidence.

## 5. Minimum information and remote technical limits

Gather only what advances the decision: customer contact, vehicle identification appropriate to the stage, affected area, symptom or damage, urgency, useful photos, relevant insurance context and material constraints. Formal customer/vehicle registration for work-order creation follows the **approved OS process**, not an assumed lead requirement.

Photo-based evaluation can help frame likely repair paths or prepare a conditional quotation. It cannot conclusively determine hidden damage, structural condition, the final scope, price or deadline where inspection is necessary. Distinguish:

- **Formal quotation:** commercially issued scope and terms with stated assumptions and legal conditions.
- **Preliminary estimate/guidance:** an initial nondefinitive view with unresolved technical or pricing dependencies.
- **Inspection required:** material uncertainty prevents a responsible formal commitment.

Do not impose an unnecessary physical visit; do not remove an essential inspection to accelerate conversion. Inspection fees, appointment conditions and inspection capacity are **not presumed** and must be confirmed before being offered.

## 6. Quotations: clarity, comparability and traceability

The customer must be able to identify:

- **What:** approved scope, covered parts/services, repairs versus replacement and material exclusions.
- **How much:** total price, payment conditions and any stated assumptions or conditions.
- **When:** realistic execution window or honest uncertainty and external dependencies.
- **What may change:** hidden damage, parts, authorization, scope and the process for reapproval.
- **How to proceed:** a simple next step, clear quote validity when defined and acceptance route.

Connect the **commercial service description** to the **technical stages** and approved work order without requiring customers to interpret production nomenclature. Keep the quote version, author, issue date and subsequent authorization traceable in the approved system. Implementation formulas and database names belong in `07` and system specifications.

**No invented quote precision:** the developing service/energy-factor pricing model does not establish verified labor hours, total cost or contribution by itself. Before expanding structured or voice-assisted automated quotation, calibrate relevant factors against actual work, costs and quality with `02`, `05` and `07` (live item `GOV-06`). An automated recommendation does not override required human/technical review.

## 7. Commercial pricing policy — what is decided and what is not

FUNIX aims to offer an economically attractive, technically responsible repair through lower waste and better operations—not through systematic margin sacrifice or a guarantee to always be cheapest.

**Division of ownership:**

| Question | Responsible conceptual file / approval |
| --- | --- |
| Costing rules, contribution analysis, cash implications and defensible economic limits | `02_business_model.md`; binding floors and assumptions require leadership approval |
| Customer price presentation, comparison protocol, discount rules, alternatives and commercial exceptions | **This file (`04`)**; execution authority must be approved and recorded |
| Technical method and acceptable alternatives | `05_operations.md` with qualified technical authority; consumer/consent safeguards in `06` |
| Escalation, authorization and decision recording | `09_decision_framework.md` and live authority/decision register |

**Current policy status:** no universal discount percentage, contribution floor, competitor-matching rule, financing option, or individual authority level is established by this text. Do not invent one or use a past example as an approved threshold. See `GOV-02`, `GOV-06` and `GOV-07`.

**Interim discount procedure:** (1) check original scope and pricing inputs; (2) identify the customer's constraint and any comparable evidence; (3) check technical alternatives and available validated economic information; (4) obtain approval from the **actually authorized commercial decision-maker** for any exception; (5) communicate the decision without false urgency and record the final authorized amount, terms and quote version. In the absence of approval, **do not promise or apply the concession**; inform the customer that the proposal remains as issued or is pending review, and preserve the option to decline. No default delegation or approval deadline is created here.

**Alternative offers** may modify a genuinely optional service, repair method, materials, timing or payment conditions **only where technically valid, legal, operationally available and explicitly disclosed/authorized**. Never disguise a quality reduction, remove essential work, misrepresent parts or quietly expand scope.

## 8. Price objections and comparable market evidence

Take the customer's price reaction seriously. It may reflect a higher comparable FUNIX quote, a genuine budget limit, payment timing, different scopes, uncertain confidence in workmanship, another purchase priority or missing information. **These explanations can coexist.** Ask without interrogating and avoid diagnosing motives as facts.

For useful comparison, record **three distinct layers**:

1. **Stated reason:** the customer's own words, including “price,” “not now,” “other provider,” or **unknown**.
2. **Comparability:** competitor value and available evidence, differences in parts/method, painted areas, damage/finish, warranty, timing, taxes/payment and exclusions; classify *equivalent, partially comparable or not verifiable*.
3. **Actual outcome when known:** chose FUNIX, chose another provider, deferred, stopped responding or unknown; do not invent competitor selection or savings.

A claimed difference of 10%, 15%, 30% or 50% in an individual case is **an observation, not a general market tolerance or a verified benchmark**. Do not publish a market-price advantage from anecdotal or noncomparable offers. Use comparable cohorts **by repair family** and note missing evidence; `02` interprets unit economics, `03` the consumer hypothesis, and `08`/`06` any public claim.

**Handling routine objections:** clarify only necessary facts; explain material differences without attacking competitors; acknowledge where FUNIX is genuinely less competitive on comparable scope; offer valid choices within approved terms; accept a respectful refusal. A discount is a decision, **not** the automatic answer to a price complaint.

## 9. Other objections, follow-up and learning

For **deadline concerns**, confirm actual urgency and operation-controlled commitments; do not invent slot availability. For **quality/trust concerns**, use authentic examples, explicit scope, process evidence and suitable guarantees, without unverified claims. For **insurance/third-party concerns**, separate FUNIX decisions from external approvals, reimbursement or payment.

Follow-up should answer a real outstanding question, clarify a difference or invite an easy resumption. It should not employ repeated pressure, guilt, manufactured scarcity or scripted emotional manipulation. The interval, frequency and actual effect of follow-ups are **testing questions**, not fixed universal cadence in this document.

**Learning from losses:** do not equate “lost due to price” with verified below-market competitiveness or infer that confidence explains every refusal. Use the three-layer record above; track unknown outcomes as unknown. Review patterns by family, channel, comparable scope and period before changing pricing or communication.

## 10. Approval, change control and operational handoff

Before execution, confirm and preserve the **accepted scope/version, price, payment terms, relevant exclusions, delivery assumptions, approvals and important dependencies**. A customer approval to repair one scope never authorizes undisclosed cost, parts, deadline or service changes. Follow `06` for consent, consumer rights, warranties and documentation requirements.

The handoff to `05` must include customer/vehicle identification, approved affected parts and services, known damage versus uninspected assumptions, parts and insurer dependencies, dates actually promised, customer mobility constraints, special conditions and unresolved technical decisions. **Sales may not create a delivery promise that the current production system cannot support.**

A material change discovered in production returns through an explicit technical/commercial/customer authorization route, and the updated commitment is recorded. An ERP status or AI-generated summary does not replace an approval. Where the promised date becomes unreliable, tell the customer promptly and provide the next defensible update.

## 11. Measurement and current commercial constraint

Use the shared metric definitions in `09_decision_framework.md`, Section 6, **rather than inventing departmental denominators**. Relevant measures include:

- time to first **useful** response and to actionable quote (event boundaries to be confirmed);
- issued quote volume and **approval rate across all genuine issued quotes**, with duplicates/tests excluded and reported separately; pending and expired cases remain visible by cohort;
- comparable quote position by repair family, including confidence/comparability;
- reasons for loss in the three-layer record and uncertainty status;
- approved jobs awaiting production, material/insurer/cash blockers and available unassigned critical-resource time;
- commercial price overrides and concessions with approver and actual economic effect when known;
- promised dates and customer-facing deviations, coordinated with `05`.

Avoid declaring the conversion funnel “the constraint” merely because sales are weak: compare valid incoming opportunities, approval outcomes, work waiting for a restricted stage, resource availability, cash and external dependencies (`09` Section 5.1; `10`). A high conversion rate that sells unprofitable or unexecutable work is not success.

Measure where decisions are being made; do not burden every interaction with unnecessary fields. Keep **only one live backlog/decision log**, in `FUNIX_REGISTROS_VIVOS.md`, Section C, with `10` coordinating enterprise priorities.

## 12. CRM, AI and safeguards

Commercial CRM/ERP, Coda, n8n, WhatsApp workflows, quote versions, customer/vehicle history, automatic stages and AI assistance are **design and development context**, not proof of live functionality. Verify each capability and its accountable record authority in `07` and the live register.

Technology should minimize unnecessary data collection, duplicated entry, repeated questioning, quote errors, missed follow-ups and unapproved transitions. For quote-to-OS handoff, protect uniqueness, approval status, change history and human accountability.

AI may support triage, drafting, summaries, alternative exploration and preliminary quote preparation, but may **not** fabricate diagnosis, prices, contribution, competitor offers, parts availability or capacity; silently apply discounts; bind the company to an unapproved quote; or create an unauthorized work order or scope change. Follow `06` for privacy and veracity and `07` for implementation controls.

## 13. Interim commercial decisions and ownership

Until formal policies and current metrics are validated:

- **Operate the current actually authorized commercial process**, while preserving legal and technical controls; do not assume the v0.3.1 proposal itself authorizes a new practice.
- **Escalate discounts/price exceptions** under `09` Section 7; customer-facing commitments remain unchanged without approval, and material pending decisions are communicated promptly.
- **Record** meaningful comparable-price evidence, stated objections and actual outcomes without inventing motives or market facts.
- **Do not scale** uncalibrated pricing/voice-quote automation into binding commercial promises (`GOV-06`).
- **Route** quality uncertainties to the qualified technical authority (`05`, `09`); no discount or urgency can authorize unsafe or unapproved repair.

Existing cross-functional open items: `GOV-02` (commercial authorities/discounts), `GOV-06` (cost and effort calibration), and `GOV-07` (market comparison/pricing). The live register contains the **single** pending-work record, including the responsible person and review date when designated.

**Operating intent:** Be practical, fast, fair and responsible. Win work because FUNIX can solve the problem with consistent quality and compelling real value—not because it pressures, disguises uncertainty, or offers an unsustainable price.
