# FUNIX — Strategic Decision Framework

## 1. Purpose

This document defines **how FUNIX decides when legitimate objectives compete**: quality, price, contribution, demand, productivity, delivery, resilience, cash, and capital.

`00_master_index.md` is the sole owner of document precedence, information-status categories, and concept ownership. `10_strategy.md` owns the central business thesis and the current order of strategic priorities. This file defines **trade-offs, decision discipline, authority logic, and measurement vocabulary**, not duplicate operating procedures.

It is not a legal opinion, a technical repair manual, or evidence that performance targets or delegated spending limits have already been adopted.

## 2. Master decision rule

**Among alternatives permitted by the binding limits in Section 3, choose the option expected to create the greatest sustainable economic value, taking account of cash, risk, reliability, customer value, and organizational learning.**

FUNIX prefers to create value through increased operational efficiency, but a sound choice may also improve demand, pricing, service mix, continuity, or capacity utilization without directly increasing labor productivity.

The decision method must be **proportional**: use existing evidence for low-risk, reversible improvements; require stronger evidence and authorization for irreversible, expensive, safety-critical, or customer-affecting actions.

Do not require a metric that does not yet exist as a condition for every decision. When measurement is incomplete, identify assumptions, choose a reversible and conservative path where permissible, define how to observe its effect, and escalate when the downside cannot be responsibly controlled.

## 3. Hard boundaries and precedence

The binding sequence is defined in `00_master_index.md`, Section 5. No financial upside overrides:

- applicable law, mandatory technical requirements, occupational and customer safety;
- honest claims, informed approvals, valid agreements, and consumer rights;
- integrity of visible **and hidden** repair work;
- technically applicable and agreed quality/scope requirements;
- lawful and responsible treatment of people and protection of customer information.

The specific legal and policy interpretation belongs to `06_legal_and_policy.md`; technical acceptance criteria belong to `05_operations.md`.

### Interim quality rule while criteria remain incomplete

Until `05_operations.md` defines approved acceptance criteria by repair family, apply mandatory manufacturer/technical requirements where applicable, the authorized repair scope, sound workmanship, and documented inspection before release. **The absence of a numeric tolerance is never permission to lower quality.** Any suspected safety, functional, hidden, durability, fitment, or finish nonconformity—including one a customer could reasonably perceive or one inconsistent with the quote—requires evaluation by the **named technical authority** in `FUNIX_REGISTROS_VIVOS.md`, Section B, before it can be marked compliant. **Do not reclassify a possible defect as optional refinement** to gain speed or margin.

If the technical authority has not been designated or cannot decide, **do not mark unresolved work as approved for delivery**; promptly inform the customer of the uncertainty and next step, document the case, and obtain technical and, when relevant, legal guidance before any requested handover. This is a quality-approval rule, not an authorization to disregard the customer's legal rights. `05` owns the final criteria, inspection roles, acceptance evidence, and handling of exceptions.

## 4. Decision method: define → constrain → compare → decide → verify

1. **Identify the decision and active constraint.** Is the limiting factor demand/conversion, production, parts, staffing, equipment, cash, quality, or management attention? Do not assume one universal constraint.
2. **Establish facts and status.** Read the dated operational register; separate measured results, observations, estimates, and hypotheses. If a key fact is unavailable, state the uncertainty.
3. **List feasible options and binding conditions.** Include the current method as a baseline; reject unsafe or unauthorized options at this stage.
4. **Estimate the whole-system effect.** Consider incremental revenue, costs, cash, contribution, time, WIP, first-pass quality, customers, skills coverage, capital and second-order bottlenecks.
5. **Choose proportionate evidence.** A reversible scheduling change can be tried with simple measurements. An expensive machine, price policy, major layout, or hiring commitment needs a documented economic case and approval.
6. **Assign decision authority and record it.** Record the rationale, decision, affected service/customer, owner, evidence, scope, review date and reversal/stop conditions.
7. **Verify and standardize.** Check real results; record the test sample and material confounders before interpreting a change as successful. Keep, adapt or reverse. A successful pilot is not a universal policy until the relevant owner approves its broader scope.

When alternatives are not comparable, identify what would make the choice resolvable; do not invent weighted scores to conceal missing data.

## 5. Essential trade-off principles

### 5.1. Demand versus production capacity

Improved technical productivity matters economically only if it creates additional profitable accepted work, avoids cost, improves service mix, protects reliability, or provides another demonstrable benefit.

If demand or quote approval is the constraint, test acquisition, response, scope clarity, price position, conversion and mix before expanding production capacity. If production is the constraint, test flow, setup, rework, readiness and bottlenecks before simply hiring.

**The correct priority follows the current constraint**, which may shift across repair families or periods. Other improvements may proceed if low-risk and worthwhile, but should not be represented as solving the active bottleneck without evidence.

### 5.2. Volume versus contribution

Neither the highest ticket, highest margin percentage, nor greatest number of cars is automatically the best choice. Consider incremental contribution, scarce-resource time, collection/payment terms, rework risk, cash and delivery effects.

Contribution per hour of a constrained resource is useful **when the constraint and the hour measure are validated**; it is not a universal score for unrelated business decisions.

Do not accept recurring work below an economically defensible level on the untested theory that volume alone will compensate.

### 5.3. Competitive price versus sustainable economics

Price-sensitive customers are legitimate prospects. Price objections may reflect genuine affordability limits, differences in scope, inadequate evidence of quality, or an actual price gap; avoid assigning a single psychological explanation.

Compare quotes only when the service scope, parts/materials, warranty, process assumptions, timing and taxes/payment terms are sufficiently comparable. Record what is not comparable.

Do not make unconditional claims that FUNIX is the cheapest or best. The definition of **competitive price** needs current matched-market evidence by repair family before being used as a substantiated public comparison.

An efficiency gain does not automatically become a discount. Decide deliberately whether its value should support lower customer price, stronger margin, reduced risk, employee capability, or reinvestment, considering competition, demand and cash. Pricing procedure and authority belong to `04_sales.md`; economic model belongs to `02_business_model.md`.

### 5.4. Quality versus avoidable effort

Required technical work, safe assembly, accepted finish and customer commitments are not waste. Correct avoidable overprocessing, defects, movement, waiting and duplicate administration first.

Quality means conforming work with consistent acceptance, not a promise of unlimited cosmetic refinement. For uncertain acceptance, apply the **single interim quality rule in Section 3**; `05_operations.md` will own its eventual technical replacement. Changes that remove or shorten required preparation or inspection steps require technical approval before adoption.

### 5.5. Standardization versus repair variability

Standardize the repeatable parts of diagnosis, handoffs, methods, tools and quality checks, while allowing documented technical deviations for genuinely different repairs.

Specialist lines, multi-skilled roles, moving vehicles, fixed bays, and hybrid cells remain **open hypotheses**. Specialization and physical vehicle movement are separate design choices. While evidence is insufficient, keep the present authorized safe arrangement, collect flow/time/quality observations, and test changes at reasonable scale.

### 5.6. Utilization versus resilience

Maximum individual utilization can harm overall output by making the workshop vulnerable to absence, failure, queueing, or inspection bottlenecks.

Evaluate backup skill coverage, documented procedures, maintenance, buffers, supervision and learning as **economic risk controls**. More employees do not automatically provide redundancy; genuinely transferable competence does.

### 5.7. Technology versus return

Evaluate machinery, software and automation on their full useful-life cost, reliability, integration, training, downtime, data protection, risk and real operational benefit. Local labor economics matter. Industrial appearance does not justify investment.

### 5.8. Expansion versus proven capability

A larger facility is a conditional option, not a goal that overrides economics. Require evidence of sustained or reachable demand, capable leadership, unit economics, cash/working capital, production flow, quality consistency, and investment return.

Facility size belongs to the dated reality register; any potential larger facility remains a long-term aspiration in `10_strategy.md`, not an approved capital plan.

### 5.9. Customer promises versus speed

Sales cannot create hidden production debt. Quotes, approvals, delivery dates and repair scope must be credible, traceable and consistent with actual capacity, parts and external dependencies. Communicate important changes promptly.

## 6. Minimal management metric dictionary

These are **provisional definitions for disciplined collection**, not certified baselines or approved performance targets. The production owner and business lead should validate event boundaries, sources and use before adopting dashboard targets.

| Metric | Proposed operational definition | Important limitation / owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Delivered vehicles | Number of vehicles formally released to customers in the chosen period | Mix-sensitive; `05` |
| Customer custody lead time | Elapsed time from vehicle check-in/receipt to technical release ready for delivery | Include pre-production waiting; record timestamps and external blockers; `05` |
| Production release-to-ready time | Elapsed time from formal production release to technical release ready for delivery | Report separately from custody lead time to avoid hiding queues; `05` |
| WIP | Vehicles with execution released but not technically released at a defined timestamp | Do not count leads or unapproved estimates as production WIP; `05` |
| Approved work awaiting production start | Approved jobs/vehicles not yet released to production at a defined timestamp | Split by vehicle available, parts, customer/insurer approval and cash constraint; `04`/`05` |
| Available but unassigned critical resource time | Hours of planned critical-resource availability without assigned productive work | Report legitimate buffers, maintenance and training separately; do not equate all idle time with waste; `05` |
| First-pass acceptance | Completed repairs accepted at the defined final inspection on the first presentation ÷ repairs presented for that inspection | Depends on approved inspection criteria and consistent recording; not yet a validated KPI; `05` |
| Post-delivery quality return | Jobs with a documented quality-related return, claim, or required correction ÷ delivered jobs in a defined follow-up cohort/window | Track timing/severity and distinguish unrelated new damage; counterweight to internal inspection; `05`/`06` |
| Rework effort | Recorded labor hours and materials to correct nonconforming original work | Separate customer-authorized scope additions, warranty work, and internal first-pass corrections; `05` |
| Labor effort | Recorded direct execution hours per repair family and task | Track paid/available time separately; don't conflate attendance with touch time; `05` |
| Equivalent production | Sum of validated standard effort units for accepted jobs or stages | **Not available as a reliable universal unit yet.** Build from sampled actual hours; energy factors used in quoting are not verified labor/cost proxies; `05` |
| Quote conversion | Quotes approved ÷ all genuine quotes issued in a defined cohort/window | Void/test duplicates excluded only with separately reported reason; report open/pending quotes distinctly; never exclude price objections as "ineligible"; `04` |
| Matched quote position | FUNIX quote compared with documented market quote(s) for equivalent scope | Do not report a precise market percentile from an unrepresentative sample; `02`/`04` |
| Job contribution | Net job revenue less attributable variable/incremental delivery and acquisition/transaction costs | Distinguish fixed payroll/overhead and allocation policy; use actual receipts/collectability where material; `02` |
| Constraint-hour contribution | Job contribution ÷ hours of the **validated current constrained resource** consumed | Applicable only where the resource and time usage are reliable; `02`/`05` |
| Delivery reliability | Promised commitments met ÷ commitments due within defined reporting period | Define responsibility for customer-controlled or insurer delays; `04`/`05` |

For every adopted metric record: **definition, formula, data source, period, owner, missing-data treatment, review cadence, and decision served**. Beware of single-metric incentives that simply relocate defects, delays or costs.

## 7. Decision authority and materiality

Specific individuals, spend limits, discount bands and technical signatories **have not been designated in this conceptual framework**. Their absence must not be silently replaced by assumed authority.

| Decision class | Default rule pending an approved authority matrix |
| --- | --- |
| Immediate safety risk or potentially unsafe repair | Stop or contain the unsafe activity; notify qualified technical/safety authority promptly. Never wait for economic approval to prevent harm. |
| Routine safe adjustment within existing approved standards | Operational responsible person may execute within their **actual documented delegation**; log important learning. |
| Scope, price, material deadline or guarantee change affecting a customer | Require documented customer approval and internal commercial/technical authorization as applicable; do not silently alter commitments. |
| Discount outside an approved pricing policy, new price floor or material loss risk | No presumed autonomy; refer to authorized FUNIX leadership/commercial owner. |
| Significant investment, staffing structure, irreversible layout or new service | Prepare a short business/risk case; leadership approval before commitment. |
| Legal, labor, privacy or regulated issue | Escalate to qualified professional and authorized FUNIX decision-maker. |
| Conflicting policy sources | Apply `00` precedence; suspend unapproved deviation and record a decision request. |

Use **materiality** to choose review intensity: (a) safety and legality, (b) customer impact/authorization, (c) money and cash exposure, (d) reversibility, (e) scope and duration, and (f) probability and severity of failure. Numerical thresholds remain to be approved; the framework never invents them.

If a matter falls into multiple classes or materiality levels, apply **the more restrictive route**. Pending a response from the authorized decision-maker, preserve the existing lawful and technically safe status (without silently changing price, scope, deadlines, or technical acceptance), inform affected customers promptly about material impact, and record the pending decision. No waiting period or substitute signatory is invented here; leadership must explicitly approve these in the authority matrix, **starting with routine discounts and technical release exceptions**. Until roles are formally assigned, escalate important decisions to FUNIX leadership and identify the specific decision-maker in the decision record. This is a temporary routing rule, not formal delegation.

## 8. Lightweight decision record

For choices with meaningful risk, investment, change in standard, or uncertainty, capture a lightweight entry in **`FUNIX_REGISTROS_VIVOS.md`, Section C** (the same table also holds open work):

`ID | Date | Problem / current constraint | Options | Binding conditions | Relevant evidence and data status | Economic/customer/quality effects | Responsible decision-maker | Decision/approval | Trial limits | Review date | Result and next action`

An ordinary reversible adjustment need not generate a lengthy memo. Escalated or irreversible decisions require more evidence and traceability.

## 9. Keeping the open work manageable

`10_strategy.md` owns the priority and sequencing of outstanding validation work. The **single live backlog and decision log** resides in `FUNIX_REGISTROS_VIVOS.md`, Section C. Do not create another list of unresolved decisions here; refer to the relevant item ID. Prioritize by business exposure, urgency, information value and evidence needed, without blocking worthwhile low-risk reversible improvements.

## 10. AI and management safeguards

- Do not optimize for raw car count, appearance of busyness, technology prestige, or a single margin percentage.
- Do not treat preference for specialists, large premises, or sophisticated software as an adopted operating mandate.
- Do not turn customer price sensitivity into an automatic diagnosis of poor customer fit.
- Do not convert a provisional metric into a proven economic fact.
- Do not bypass technical quality, approval, safety, labor or privacy controls for speed or unit-cost savings.
- If crucial data is missing, explain the decision that cannot yet be made confidently and offer the smallest useful observation or reversible test.
- Keep the full enterprise objective in view: **sustainable economic value produced through reliable repairs, meaningful productivity, responsible pricing and continuous learning**.
