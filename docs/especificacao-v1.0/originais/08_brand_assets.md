# FUNIX — Brand Assets & Execution Context

**Release and asset status:** Refer to `FUNIX_REGISTROS_VIVOS.md` for the approved release and availability of master brand assets. This file is **conceptual guidance**, not a finished identity manual or proof that a logo, font, campaign, website or color specification has been formally approved.

## 1. Purpose and boundaries

This file owns **brand expression and presentation** across website, messages, campaigns, images, printed forms, signage, uniforms and customer/employee interfaces. Enduring company identity belongs to `01_identity.md`; customer-research hypotheses to `03_customer_psychology.md`; selling and quoting procedure to `04_sales.md`; technical claims, consents and public-claim defensibility to `06_legal_and_policy.md`; website infrastructure, analytics and integration to `07_digital.md`.

Creative work must express actual value, not create an appearance of sophistication unsupported by the service. Performance, legibility, authenticity and clarity are part of the brand experience.

## 2. Desired expression: capable, straightforward, accessible

FUNIX seeks to convey:

- real technical competence and work carried out responsibly;
- organized, predictable, low-friction customer dealings;
- calm clarity about what will be done, why and under which conditions;
- a practical, accessible alternative to both luxury theater and visibly improvised repair;
- a compelling **price-to-result relationship**, not a public promise to be universally cheapest.

The internal *“amigo engenheiro”* metaphor means a knowledgeable, approachable person who explains and resolves without showing off. It is **not** a claim about an employee's credential or a mandatory customer-facing slogan.

**Do not equate an expensive-looking brand with good work or a simple-looking brand with low quality.** Visual refinement should make the service easier to understand, not signal exclusivity.

## 3. Proof and public-claim discipline

Use authentic, appropriately authorized examples of the shop, technicians, process, parts, quality checks, repairs and handover. Where useful, show the real reason for technical choices in plain language. Before/after imagery must be representative and must not materially alter or disguise the result.

Do not invent testimonials, disguise reference or stock photography as FUNIX operations, fake reviews, assert “market leader,” “best,” “most reliable,” “unbeatable price” or imply measured defect/productivity advantages without evidence. Competitive pricing may be communicated with **specific, correctly scoped and current evidence** when validated by `02`/`04` and reviewed under `06`.

Communicating quality **does not require luxury imagery**. Likewise, hiding price completely can create an unnecessarily expensive perception. Test simple, truthful ways to express practical value and enable a clear quote request, without discount theater or aggressive claims.

## 4. Working visual references, subject to approved assets

The original brand references are preserved pending checking against actual master files:

| Reference | Working color | Intended use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deep blue | `#0A1F44` | Trust, technical seriousness, brand continuity. |
| White | `#FFFFFF` | Clarity, legibility, space and cleanliness. |
| Orange | `#FF6A00` | Controlled emphasis, practical action and attention. |

Blue generally anchors the identity, white provides readability, orange highlights actions or exceptions. Avoid aggressive overuse of orange, claustrophobic dark backgrounds and excessive decorative gradients, chrome, flames, racing flags, carbon-fiber textures and exaggerated sports-car motifs.

**These hex values are inherited working references, not verified official vector artwork, print CMYK/Pantone values or accessibility certification.** Final color matching, approved logo proportions, clear space, minimum sizes, fonts and variants require the actual authorized master assets. Do not redraw or reinterpret a missing logo from text alone.

The original symbolic direction—**damage → correction → restored confidence**—can inform execution without demanding a literal pictogram. Reject racing, cartoon, hyper-luxury and generic mechanic-icon reinterpretations unless formally reviewed.

## 5. Typography and graphic language

Use highly legible typography and consistent hierarchy across phones, A4 forms, quotations, dashboards and signage. Exact font families, weights and tokens must be checked against approved assets, not invented.

Prioritize:

- short, meaningful headings; clear labels and numbers;
- clean alignment, spacing and controlled technical geometry;
- contrast, comfortable reading and status indicators not conveyed by color alone;
- information in order of user need rather than decorative importance;
- reusable components that reduce variation and production rework.

Avoid ornamental lettering, racing-style fonts, excessive jargon, gratuitous motion and decoration that impairs printing, data entry or scanning.

## 6. Photography, video and real operational evidence

Capture actual facilities, clear examples of workmanship, authentic technicians, appropriate tools, staged repairs and finished vehicles. Be truthful about the date and stage of work. Maintain identifiable differences among **service documentation**, **training material** and **public advertising**; permissions appropriate for one use do not automatically authorize another.

Useful subjects include: real damage and resulting finish; process controls rather than posed spectacle; the human explanation of an estimate; documented receipt/delivery where permission permits. Do not use staged images to imply a machine, workshop capability or level of organization that FUNIX has not actually deployed.

Before/after comparisons should use fair framing and avoid materially misleading lighting, crops, retouching or color treatment. Customer/vehicle identification requires privacy and consent review where relevant (`06`).

## 7. Messaging and customer language

Working message hierarchy:

1. **Main:** we understand and can address the specific repair problem within an appropriate method and scope.
2. **Support:** the customer can expect clear explanations, a coherent process and realistic commitments.
3. **Economic:** explain value through actual scope and conditions, using verified comparable pricing when available.
4. **Internal mechanism:** efficiency, standard work and learning produce the desired business advantage; these need not be advertised as a slogan.

Preferred concepts in Brazilian Portuguese include: *reparo adequado, solução, clareza, responsabilidade, confiança, cuidado, organização, orçamento, prazo, qualidade do serviço*. Avoid habitual use of unverified superiority terms (*melhor da cidade, qualidade incomparável, imbatível, perfeito, mais barato*), luxury/exclusivity clichés and artificial emotional storytelling.

Example **illustrative** invitations (not approved campaigns): “Conte o que aconteceu com seu carro”; “Envie fotos para uma orientação inicial”; “Solicite uma avaliação”; “Entenda o serviço proposto”. The actual CTA should match the real route and technical limits in `04`, be legally supportable under `06`, and work in the deployed channel in `07`.

## 8. Website, ads and customer experience

The public site should be **mobile-first, useful and credible**, with visible contact/location, accurate services, authentic evidence, sensible speed, readable information and simple next steps. `07_digital.md` owns domains, tracking, SEO infrastructure and live acquisition channels. `08` owns presentation, tone, imagery, brand consistency and creative usability.

Ads and content should help prospects recognize a relevant problem and evaluate a plausible solution; avoid faking urgency, claiming perfect turnaround, excessively technical lectures or showing luxury vehicles as if they were the core customer proposition.

Social content can feature genuine work, team members, practical explanations, progress and customer questions. Do not post only motivational material, generic stock imagery or cosmetic content that obscures actual output.

## 9. Operational documents are brand touchpoints

The brand is experienced in the **checklist, quotation, authorization, customer updates and delivery record**, not merely in advertising. A good document is quick to understand, easy to complete, difficult to misread and suitable for the actual device or paper format.

For FUNIX checklists, preserve approved vehicle outlines and inspection elements; **do not add illustrated body lines or markings that are not in the technical reference solely to make them look richer**. Design should support adequate handwriting space, high contrast and practical scanning or printing, including A4 where required. Functional correctness takes precedence over embellishment.

Quotes should foreground service scope, totals, conditions and next action; an operator dashboard should foreground actionable information rather than visually impressive charts. Signs and uniforms should be clear, durable and fit the real work environment.

## 10. Governance and lightweight creative review

Before releasing a material, check:

- **Truth:** statements, price comparisons, testimonials and service evidence are supportable (`06`).
- **Relevance:** the customer understands the actual repair proposition (`03`, `04`).
- **Accuracy:** correct logo/master assets, colors, terms, vehicle depiction and scope.
- **Usability:** reading, contact actions, accessibility and printed completion work in practice.
- **Capacity:** promises, availability and service offerings reflect reality (`05`, `07`).
- **Ownership:** the responsible person approves the applicable material, with a record where risk warrants it.

Do not turn creative approval into a long chain for every harmless micro-adjustment. Review depth should reflect claim, cost, customer and reputational risk (`09`). Keep a controlled master-asset location; record approved versions rather than circulating contradictory templates.

## 11. Remaining open decisions

Verified logo files, official typefaces, production/print values, approved messaging examples, signage specifications and any public comparative price claims still require actual asset/evidence review. Track decisions and accountable people **once in `FUNIX_REGISTROS_VIVOS.md`, Section C**; this file does not create a parallel backlog.

**Brand rule:** show genuine competence, practical clarity and accessible value. Let operational excellence be demonstrated by the work and experience—not by visual theater or unsupported marketing promises.
