## 2. Strategic situation: what we know and what we do not

FUNIX is an automotive body-repair and paint business in Contagem, Minas Gerais, building a more institutionalized management and operating system on top of practical service experience.

The prior description of the company as having no structured foundations has become too simplistic. Work has been developed on quoting, technical service stages, intake documentation, order management, Coda/n8n and related automation. **Design, prototype, partial deployment, stable operation, and measured benefit are distinct states.** Their present status belongs to the dated live register, not to a timeless strategy claim.

The following are **working concerns**, not a measured diagnosis of today's dominant constraint:

- repair-to-repair variation in damage, scope, parts, complexity, and lead time;
- sensitivity to price together with expectations of proper technical quality;
- potential waiting, handoff, rework, quality and scheduling losses;
- dependence on specific professionals for some capabilities;
- variable demand, quote conversion, channel economics and cash requirements;
- the growing attention burden of supervising people, processes and systems.

The reported disruption following the absence of a polishing professional is meaningful evidence of a **possible single-person vulnerability**, not proof of its frequency or of the ideal staffing solution.

**Open diagnostic question:** which factor currently limits sustainable economic results — demand and conversion, repair capacity, parts/approvals, cash, quality, management bandwidth, or a combination? It may differ by repair family and over time. The first short factual baseline should answer that question before a fixed intervention order is adopted. See live item `GOV-03`.

## 3. Strategic north star and business thesis

**North star:** build a sustainably profitable and resilient body-repair and paint business whose operating system delivers technically responsible, consistently accepted repairs at compelling customer value, with increasing output per relevant resource and capacity to reinvest.

**Efficiency is FUNIX's preferred path to competitive advantage, not its ultimate economic objective.** Sustainable value creation, reliable service, liquidity, and continuity are the objective. Growth is warranted when it strengthens these outcomes; volume alone does not.

### 3.1. The mechanism we intend to prove

1. **Understand the work and the market:** classify recurring repair families; map customer demand, quote acceptance, work content, lead times, and the real constraint.
2. **Stabilize acceptable quality:** establish technical and commercial scope, repeatable methods, inspection points, and the competencies required to deliver reliably.
3. **Remove avoidable loss:** reduce waiting, unnecessary movements, preventable rework, unclear handoffs, poor scheduling, and administrative duplication without removing necessary technical work.
4. **Improve productive and economic output:** deliver more comparable accepted work with the same relevant resources where feasible, or avoid genuine costs and risks.
5. **Capture the gain:** determine deliberately how benefits appear as competitive prices, stronger contribution, better delivery reliability, capacity flexibility, workforce capability, or reinvestment.
6. **Sustain and repeat:** train, measure, correct, document and improve the operating method; expand capacity only when demand, cash, management and economics support it.

This is a **conditional causal thesis**, not a promised flywheel. Productivity can create idle time rather than profit; price reductions can fail to convert more demand; extra utilization can create queues; new equipment can shift rather than remove a bottleneck. No stage is presumed successful without observed benefit.

### 3.2. Conditions for a defensible advantage

The thesis becomes economically credible only if the following hold for a meaningful portion of actual work:

| Condition to demonstrate | Strategic implication if it is not true |
| --- | --- |
| A material share of controllable job cost can be improved without reducing required quality. | Look to sourcing, service mix, pricing, demand, or other genuine value levers; do not promise cost leadership through labor productivity alone. |
| Enough repairs share repeatable tasks or can be grouped into practical families. | Use selective standards and flexible expertise rather than forcing one universal line. |
| Waiting, setup, defects or administrative effort contain recoverable loss. | Avoid large transformation programs without a real opportunity. |
| Approved demand, preventable spending, or profitable alternative use can absorb recovered capacity. | Prioritize conversion/demand, avoidable cost, or resilience before adding production capacity. |
| Savings remain after training, supervision, quality control, capital costs and rework. | Reject apparent improvements that relocate cost or risk. |
| The customer experiences value, and the firm retains enough benefit to finance operations and renewal. | Adjust the offer, price strategy, service mix, or gain allocation. |

A potential long-term advantage may come from accumulated repair-family data, trained teams, documented operating knowledge, dependable delivery, trust earned through results, and disciplined management. **None of these is claimed to be a durable moat already achieved**; competitors can imitate individual practices.

## 4. Priority logic: act on the binding constraint

FUNIX should not treat the numbered priorities of the v0.2 proposal as a fixed project schedule. There are **always-on protections** and **conditional improvement priorities**.

### 4.1. Always-on requirements

Maintain safe and technically responsible work, customer-authorized scope, contractual and legal commitments, defensible inspection and release, employee safety, truthful communication, and minimum visibility into cash and delivery. These requirements are constraints, not optional projects ranked below growth.

Until repair-family acceptance standards are defined in `05_operations.md`, follow the **single interim quality rule** in `09_decision_framework.md`, Section 3. In material technical uncertainty, do not approve a vehicle for release without the necessary technical decision; communicate appropriately and respect applicable consumer rights. The responsible authority must be explicitly designated in the live register (`GOV-01`, `GOV-04`).

### 4.2. Diagnose, then prioritize

Use the decision method in `09_decision_framework.md`, Section 4. Diagnose from simple indicators before allocating significant resources:

| Current limiting condition | Initial strategic response | Avoid assuming |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Insufficient profitable approved demand** or unassigned critical capacity | Examine lead quality, response, quote clarity, scope comparability, market-relative price, conversion, channels and customer experience; run small tests. | More technicians, a larger building, or a faster repair step will create demand. |
| **Approved work waiting to start / congested production** | Examine readiness, parts, WIP, handoffs, stage bottlenecks, rework, quality control, shift/skill coverage and current scheduling before increasing fixed capacity. | Every workstation or technician needs to be fully occupied. |
| **Parts, insurer/customer approvals or outside dependencies** | Expose the source of waiting; adjust intake readiness, procurement, agreements, communication and planning within legitimate control. | Worker speed solves an externally blocked vehicle. |
| **Cash or working capital constraint** | Review collections, payment terms, purchasing commitments, contribution, cash timing and capital requirements before increasing throughput commitments. | Positive quoted margin means sufficient cash. |
| **Quality failure / recurring rework** | Contain technical risk, investigate root causes and restore acceptable first-pass performance before scaling output. | Defects are an acceptable price of growth. |
| **Dependence on one person / insufficient leadership capacity** | Map essential skills, approved substitutions, supervision and process knowledge; assess training, backup, recruitment and workload economically. | Hiring alone creates redundancy or good management. |

More than one factor may constrain results. Prioritize by customer/safety exposure, impact on sustainable economic value, urgency, available evidence, reversibility and management capacity. A safe, low-cost improvement can proceed outside the dominant constraint if it has a clear benefit, but must not be described as resolving that constraint.

**If facts are insufficient:** use a short, proportionate observation window and record uncertainty rather than asserting a diagnosis. No universal duration or numeric threshold is imposed here.

### 4.3. Minimum evidence before material reallocation

Focus the first baseline on **a few linked views**, by recent period and repair family where practical:

- **Demand:** leads, genuine quotes issued, approvals, lost/pending quotes, comparable competitor quotations, and main loss reasons.
- **Flow and quality:** accepted deliveries, approved jobs waiting to start, WIP, custody/production lead times, critical-resource availability, rework, inspection results and post-delivery quality returns.
- **Economics and liquidity:** attributable variable costs, contribution by family/channel where reliable, payment/collection timing, and cash constraints.
- **Organization:** named technical/price decision roles, critical skills coverage, and adoption status of relevant digital flows.

Definitions and data-quality caveats belong to the metric dictionary in `09`, Section 6; factual entries and approval status belong to the live register. **The quoting “energy” factor is not a validated measure of hours, equivalent output, or actual cost.** Calibration is necessary before it becomes the basis for reliable unit-cost or automated-price claims (`GOV-06`).

## 5. Commercial demand and price/value strategy

The proposed customer proposition combines **technically appropriate quality, approachable treatment, transparent scope, reliable fulfillment, and a price that is competitive for comparable work**. It is neither a luxury brand nor a low-quality discount operation.

Customers can be highly price sensitive without wanting inferior work. A rejected quote can reflect real price differences, affordability, different scope, timing, perceived risk, or inadequate proof of quality. Do not presume a single cause. Research belongs to `03`; quotation comparison and policy belong to `04` (`GOV-02`, `GOV-07`).

**Working acquisition priority:** capture high-intent local repair demand (including Google/Business Profile, WhatsApp, referrals and returning customers) where contribution and execution fit are favorable. Preserve authentic evidence of work and clear communication. Other acquisition experiments, including paid social or aesthetics, remain conditional on measured returns, cash and production compatibility.

**Institutional channels:** insurers, associations, fleets and partners may stabilize utilization or provide valuable work. Do not assume retail always wins, nor accept volume solely to fill the shop. Compare each channel on actual contribution, constrained capacity, working capital, contractual control of price/scope, delivery risk and customer concentration. Avoid dependence on a single payer where the risk outweighs its benefit. Economics are defined in `02`; partnership/contract protections in `06`.

**Price discipline:** aim for an economically compelling offer, not guaranteed market leadership. Price comparisons need matched repair scope, materials/parts, quality, warranty, timelines and relevant payment conditions. A cost improvement may support lower price, stronger margin, resilience, employee capability or reinvestment; the distribution is a management decision based on demand, competition, cash and service economics, not an automatic discount.

The historical R$70,000–R$150,000 vehicle band is a **past segmentation hypothesis**, not a current qualifying rule. Service fit, achievable quality, economic viability and customer needs are more useful working filters. The home market remains Contagem and economically serviceable parts of Greater Belo Horizonte; geography and campaign design are execution questions, not separate enterprise theses.

## 6. Production, people and technology: strategic choices still open

### 6.1. Industrial discipline adapted to repair variability

FUNIX may learn from methodical management (including Vicente Falconi), lean production, specialist workflows and industrial treatment of variable inputs. **These references are sources of techniques, not evidence that a specific layout works here.** A repaired vehicle has distinct damage, scope, owner, custody and delivery commitments.

The following **remain unselected hypotheses**:

- specialist versus multi-skilled roles, or hybrid repair cells;
- stationary vehicles with mobile specialists versus vehicles moving between stations;
- standard routing by repair family versus case-specific routing;
- manual, assisted or automated tools where local economics justify them.

Maintain the currently authorized safe operating method while collecting comparable flow, effort, quality, cost and resilience observations. Use low-risk trials where suitable; evaluate larger changes with a proportionate business case and technical validation. Test specialization and vehicle movement separately rather than assuming they must come together.

### 6.2. Organizational capability and continuity

The goal is a **system that supports capable professionals**, not a culture dependent on one indispensable person. Build accessible work standards, practical training, approved cross-coverage of critical skills, defined supervision, feedback from technicians, quality control at critical points, and safe working conditions.

Do not treat technicians as interchangeable, require universal multi-skilling, or assume a larger payroll automatically reduces variability. Staffing and incentive choices need economic, operational and employment review (`05` and `06`); leadership capacity is itself a possible growth bottleneck.

### 6.3. Digital capability and capital discipline

Digital systems should connect customer, vehicle, quote, approval, work order, production, inspections, cost learning and delivery with reliable record ownership and minimal duplicate entry. Use automation to remove real effort and errors; preserve human responsibility for material technical, commercial and legal approvals.

Coda, n8n, WhatsApp and AI-related work has been discussed or developed, but **actual deployment and reliability are only those verified in the live register**. Table schemas, source code, event tracking and website implementation belong in `07`, not here.

Equipment, automation, expansion and significant hiring should be evaluated against process-only alternatives on total economic return, cash, maintenance, training, quality, continuity and downside risk (`02`, `09`). Supplier productivity claims are hypotheses until observed in FUNIX's own work.

## 7. Growth horizons and scale conditions

These horizons are **logical maturity states, not dated commitments**. Activities may overlap when sensible; no horizon is earned through paperwork alone.

| Horizon | Strategic focus | Evidence that supports moving further |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **H1 — See and stabilize the current business** | Reliable minimum demand/cost/flow facts; quality and release safeguards; critical-role coverage; clarity of present constraint; basic commercial follow-up. | Credible diagnosis and a manageable, repeatedly executed production/commercial routine. |
| **H2 — Prove repeatable advantage** | Repair-family pilots, learning from actual cost/effort, qualified demand, competitive price evidence, fewer avoidable losses, better quality/lead-time control. | Repeated conforming outcomes, sound contribution and cash, demonstrated productive improvement or other validated economic benefit. |
| **H3 — Expand proven capacity** | Extend successful operating methods, supervision and coverage; add targeted machinery, channels, workforce and space. | Sustained or credibly obtainable profitable demand, financeable working capital, mature quality control, management bandwidth and defensible investment returns. |

**Larger-facility vision:** leadership has described the possibility of a future facility around **5,000 m²**. This is an ambition, not a capital authorization, optimal footprint, or fixed milestone. Current area figures belong in the dated reality register. Any capacity case must be built from expected throughput, repair-family mix, lead time/WIP, physical space requirements, paint-booth and equipment constraints, staffing, collections and cash—not by scaling floor area mechanically.

Expansion can reduce rather than improve economics if fixed commitments arrive before demand or management capability. Broader automotive care, aesthetics, regional reach and additional sites remain options, not automatic next steps.

## 8. Strategic learning agenda and decision backlog ownership

`10_strategy.md` owns the **ranking logic and sequence** for unresolved strategic work. The **only operative list of individual pending decisions and tests** is Section C of `FUNIX_REGISTROS_VIVOS.md`; do not maintain a competing numbered backlog inside domain documents.

For prioritization, apply these considerations in order of relevance to each live decision:

1. **Protection and exposure:** safety, technical quality, contractual/legal obligations, cash continuity and customer harm require prompt attention independent of the growth thesis.
2. **Current constraint and gain:** favor actions that remove the demonstrated limiter of profitable accepted work, avoid material costs or contain a major risk.
3. **Decision value of evidence:** favor a small observation or reversible test when it can change an upcoming investment, pricing or process choice.
4. **Execution bandwidth:** sequence work that the actual management and team can implement, maintain and verify; avoid parallel projects whose maintenance costs exceed benefit.
5. **Dependencies:** measure before calibrating; define quality and technical authority before relaxing or accelerating approval steps; understand economics before approving price floors or scale commitments.

The current register already identifies proposed items `GOV-01` through `GOV-07`. Its listed priorities are provisional suggestions, not proof of today's constraint or formal management approval. Do not add duplicate lists here; adjust the live record when the authorized decision-maker validates new facts and choices.

A useful first synthesis, **once recent facts are gathered**, is a one-page answer to: *What is limiting our economic result now, what evidence supports it, what low-risk change will we test, what safeguards apply, and how will we know whether it worked?*

## 9. Risks, boundaries, and success

Watch for:

- productivity gains without demand or avoidable-cost capture;
- low prices unsupported by actual contribution and collections;
- throughput increases achieved by moving defects to warranty or hidden work;
- queues and WIP growing faster than accepted output;
- unverified quoting factors masquerading as labor or cost measures;
- dependence on individual technicians, equipment, founders or system maintainers;
- sophisticated software or equipment absorbing management time without value;
- channel/customer concentration, late collections and overcommitted fixed costs;
- standards that are written but not executed, or incentives that create unsafe shortcuts.

Success is not one metric. FUNIX should seek **reliable repair quality, satisfied and fairly treated customers, sound contribution and cash, productive use of resources, predictable delivery, learning that survives turnover, and growth that preserves or improves the model**. Quantitative KPIs, denominators, periods and approvals belong to `09` and the live record.

**Strategic test for recommendations:** does this step produce a defensible improvement in sustainable customer and economic value, within binding quality/legal limits, given the actual constraint and FUNIX's ability to execute it? If not known, prefer a smaller observation or reversible experiment over a large commitment.
