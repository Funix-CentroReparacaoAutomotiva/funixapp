# FUNIX — Core Identity Context

## 1. Purpose and boundaries

This file defines **who FUNIX is, what it stands for, how it should relate to people, and which behaviors are unacceptable**. Its enduring identity guides leadership, collaborators, and AI systems; it is not a technical repair standard, commercial pricing policy, financial target, or current-state operating report.

The precedence and release rules are defined in `00_master_index.md`. The full efficiency-led business thesis, priorities, and growth hypotheses belong to `10_strategy.md`; trade-off decisions to `09_decision_framework.md`. Technical quality acceptance belongs to `05_operations.md`, commercial pricing to `04_sales.md`, and public claims and visual expression to `06_legal_and_policy.md` and `08_brand_assets.md` respectively. No statement in this file independently approves a commercial claim, operational exception, or new policy.

## 2. Who we are

FUNIX is an automotive body-repair and paint business based in Contagem, Minas Gerais. Its identity is rooted in **technical responsibility, straightforward relationships, honest communication, and work people can rely on**.

FUNIX is not a general low-quality discount shop, an exclusive luxury brand, or a restoration atelier by default. Its intended customer proposition is practical: solve the relevant repair problem properly, with an understandable scope, reliable treatment, and compelling value. The company seeks to be **accessible without sacrificing standards**, and commercially competitive without defining itself by discounts.

Service categories, their present importance, and the economic attractiveness of each belong to `02_business_model.md` and `05_operations.md`; identity should not freeze a service mix that may evolve.

## 3. Mission

**Transform a moment of insecurity into an experience of trust.**

A vehicle repair can create uncertainty about technical work, cost, timing, mobility, or whether the customer is being told the truth. FUNIX aims to replace avoidable uncertainty with understandable decisions, responsible execution, and transparent communication.

Trust is both **a conduct obligation to earn** and **a customer perception to validate**, not an automatic consequence of a slogan. It does not, by itself, prove the company's economic advantage; the mechanisms for durable economic value are addressed in `10_strategy.md` and `02_business_model.md`.

## 4. Values and core beliefs

- **Integrity in visible and hidden work.** Quality does not become optional merely because a defect may go unnoticed.
- **Technical responsibility.** Safety, suitable methods, durable outcomes, and the authorized scope matter more than cosmetic appearances or convenient shortcuts.
- **Clarity and honesty.** Explain what is known, what remains uncertain, what is proposed, and what requires approval. Transparency should be normal practice, not theater.
- **Human competence.** Practical craftsmanship and technical knowledge must coexist with patience, accessibility, and respect.
- **Dependability.** Commit only to what can responsibly be delivered; communicate relevant deviations early.
- **Respect for people.** Customers deserve to understand their options; employees are skilled contributors, not disposable resources.
- **Disciplined learning.** Prefer observable results and honest correction over improvisation, blame, prestige, or attachment to one method.
- **Economic responsibility.** Seek value for the customer and a business capable of honoring commitments over time; avoid waste without removing necessary work.

These are enduring values. Particular efficiency mechanisms, operating layouts, price positions, and performance levels remain subjects of evidence and decision in their own documents.

## 5. Positioning and customer relationship

FUNIX should be recognized as **a capable, organized, approachable repair company that solves the problem with a clear explanation and a sensible value proposition**.

A customer can care strongly about price **and** refuse poor-quality repairs. Comparing quotations does not, by itself, make a customer unfit. Requests for concealed defects, unsafe work, unauthorized changes, or dishonest shortcuts are different: they conflict with FUNIX's values.

The original positioning phrase remains a useful identity reference, **not an automatically approved advertising slogan**:

> High standard in execution. Simple in relationships.

“High standard” means technically appropriate work that conforms to the applicable, approved service requirements—not unlimited cosmetic refinement or a claim of universal superiority. The definition and acceptance of that standard belong to `05_operations.md` and applicable technical sources.

Competitive value is an **intended position**. FUNIX must not claim to be the cheapest, best, or consistently below market without adequate current, comparable evidence and claim review (`02`, `04`, `06`, `08`).

## 6. Personality and voice

FUNIX should feel like **an experienced, practical specialist who explains clearly without making the customer feel inferior**—the approachable *“engineer friend”* as an internal metaphor, not a promise about individual qualifications.

Its voice is calm, direct, respectful, human, precise, and free of arrogance. It should make complexity understandable without trivializing real technical uncertainties.

Avoid cold corporate jargon, artificial luxury, aggressive sales pressure, exaggerated superiority, false urgency, empty slogans, and unnecessary technical displays. Do not confuse a polished presentation with evidence of sound work. Use genuine proof and plain language when explaining value.

Exact campaign copy, color, typography, imagery, channel tactics and calls to action belong to `08_brand_assets.md`; consultation, quotations and follow-up procedures belong to `04_sales.md`.

## 7. Desired customer experience

Customers should be able to feel that:

- they were listened to and understood;
- the proposed repair and its limitations were explained honestly;
- the price and scope were understandable, even when not the lowest;
- delivery commitments were treated seriously;
- problems were not hidden and material changes were communicated;
- the final result respected the approved work and applicable quality requirements.

A useful emotional direction is: **from confusion and insecurity toward clarity, confidence, and trust**. These are desired experiences, not proven rankings of what every customer values. Customer evidence and hypotheses belong to `03_customer_psychology.md`.

FUNIX does not promise perfect outcomes under every circumstance, guaranteed lowest prices, or certainty based on incomplete inspection. It commits to responsible judgment, candid communication, valid approvals, and honest correction when necessary.

## 8. Non-negotiable behavior

The following are incompatible with FUNIX's identity:

- using the excuse **“The customer will never notice”** to justify lower-quality visible or hidden work;
- concealing defects, material damage, uncertainty, or significant changes to the agreed service;
- selling unnecessary work, making unsupported claims, or taking advantage of technical information asymmetry;
- substituting an inferior or unsafe method, part, or material without the applicable technical and customer authorization;
- promising a scope, price, quality, timing, or guarantee the company cannot responsibly support;
- pressuring clients, disguising discounts, or treating budget concerns with contempt;
- pursuing throughput, lower cost, or appearance at the expense of safety, technical conformity, workers' lawful treatment, or customer rights;
- ignoring a verified problem rather than correcting or appropriately escalating it.

These conduct boundaries are **binding only when this identity document is formally approved** under `00_master_index.md`. Law, mandatory technical requirements, safety, and customer rights apply regardless of publication status.

## 9. Internal cultural identity

FUNIX respects practical expertise and aims to make good work **more consistent, teachable, and resilient**. A documented method should support professional judgment; it must not become a reason to disregard the real conditions of a vehicle or silence a technician who identifies a problem.

People should have access to clear expectations, appropriate training, feedback, and the ability to propose improvements. Errors should be investigated and corrected with accountability rather than a culture of hiding mistakes or blaming individuals.

The company seeks to reduce fragile dependence on irreplaceable individuals through learning, documentation and appropriate competence coverage, while recognizing that skills, authorizations, and staffing decisions cannot be presumed interchangeable. Detailed workforce design belongs to `05_operations.md`, employment safeguards to `06_legal_and_policy.md`, and resource decisions to `02_business_model.md` and `09_decision_framework.md`.

**The company may change its methods without changing its values.** Specialist lines, multifunctional professionals, hybrid cells, machines, automation and larger facilities remain contingent choices, not elements of identity.

## 10. Interpretation safeguard

When using FUNIX's identity to assess a recommendation, ask whether it is truthful, technically and ethically responsible, understandable to customers, respectful to people, and consistent with a dependable repair relationship.

Do not infer from this file that any particular price, cost advantage, production standard, staff capability, process, digital integration, or market perception has already been measured or achieved. Check dated facts in `FUNIX_REGISTROS_VIVOS.md` and the owning domain file; use `09_decision_framework.md` for trade-offs under uncertainty.

**Enduring identity:** competent work, honest relationships, human respect, and trust earned in practice—not luxury signaling, discount theater, or claims that outpace reality.
